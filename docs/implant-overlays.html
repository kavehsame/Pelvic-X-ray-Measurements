How implant overlays work

An implant overlay places a drawing of known size on a pelvic radiograph. Its size depends on both the scale of the manufacturer's drawing and the scale of the patient image. This guide follows both, and shows how to make and import your own overlay pack.

From PDF to transparent PNG

The OsiriX plugin pairs the manufacturers’ implant drawings with plain-text product records. [1, 2, 3] One sheet may contain several sizes or views. Keeping the entire page at this stage preserves the ruler, labels and original positions needed to identify each drawing.

  1. Keep the originals together. The conversion recorded the source files and their checksums, kept their folder hierarchy and copied every .txt file byte for byte. The text links a product reference to its drawing and may give positions on that drawing.
  2. Render the full page at 300 dots per inch (dpi). PyMuPDF rendered each visible PDF page directly to a colour PNG with a transparency channel. It used the page's physical size without a screenshot, print dialogue, cropping or resizing. One inch on the page becomes 300 pixels.
  3. Preserve the original marks. Unpainted parts of the page became transparent. A white mark deliberately painted in the PDF remained visible against a darker background. Partly transparent edge pixels kept strokes smooth. Removing every white pixel afterwards would erase some of these details.
  4. Keep page identity. A single-page PDF kept its original name with a .png ending. The one two-page Mathys PDF produced a separate PNG for each page, with _page_001 and _page_002 suffixes.
  5. Reopen and check. Every PNG was checked against its PDF page dimensions, its recorded 300 dpi and the presence of visible and transparent pixels. File checksums verified the text copies and a second copy of the PNG set. Representative pages from both manufacturers were also checked on contrasting backgrounds and compared with the source on white.

Exact conversion settings and checks

The original conversion used PyMuPDF 1.28.0. For each page it rendered the full page in RGB with transparency, using page.get_pixmap(dpi=300, colorspace=pymupdf.csRGB, alpha=True), then saved the result as PNG. Expected pixel dimensions were calculated from the PDF page in points (72 points per inch). The original PDF and PNG therefore shared the same page boundaries.

Grey-background checks included drawings from both manufacturers and both pages of the Mathys twinSys sheet. They retained the small painted white details in the Medacta B-Cage drawing. On the samples compared against a white background, no pixel differed from the source render by five or more levels on the 0–255 greyscale.

This conversion was part of preparing the catalogue. To use a prepared pack, you only need PXM.

From full page to one implant view

  1. Find the product and view. Read the manufacturer, family, size, product reference, permitted patient side and AP drawing from the associated records. AP means the front view. The review covered 895 views, including lateral and additional profiles. The PXM catalogue uses 794 AP entries from 51 families, drawn from 705 full pages.
  2. Isolate the right drawing. The OsiriX crop window locates the design on its page. The preparation keeps the ink that belongs to that view, including strokes that continue beyond the window. It excludes adjacent sizes and page furniture, trims transparent margins and leaves a narrow transparent border. Several references can legitimately share the same image.
  3. Make it visible on the X-ray. The selected PNG pixels receive a light colour. Cups are cyan, cages mint, whole stems gold, proximal stem components lilac and distal components pale green. Each retained pixel keeps its original position and opacity. Internal marks such as head centres and construction lines travel with the drawing.
  4. Record where to place it. The pivot is the point you place on the X-ray and rotate around. The preparation converts the source origin into pixels, adjusts it for the crop and records the physical size of a pixel, the side shown in the drawing and the allowed patient side. A crop removes surrounding space without changing the implant's size.

Read the scale on the implant's own sheet

Each catalogue entry points back to its original sheet. The product's printed size identifies the model. The physical scale comes from an independent reference on that sheet.

How scale was established for the 705 AP source pages
ReferencePagesWhat was checked
Printed ruler296Measure from the centres of the 0 and 100 mm marks in the PDF. Compare all 10 mm intervals against the marks in the full PNG. A longer interval reduces the influence of a single pixel at an endpoint.
Printed 1:1 scale400Use the sheet's declaration that the drawing is actual size. Check the physical PDF page dimensions against the exported PNG. At 300 dpi, one pixel on a 1:1 page represents about 0.08467 mm (25.4 ÷ 300).
Dimensioned head-centre marks9These stem pages lack a ruler and a printed ratio. Match at least three head-centre marks to the manufacturer's coordinates in millimetres, and check the marks in both the original PDF and PNG.

For a ruler, divide its known length in millimetres by its length in pixels. The resulting millimetres per pixel follow the drawing through cropping, tinting, mirroring and rotation. A reference mark can remain outside the finished crop and still establish its scale.

How OsiriX sets the size

In the OsiriX plugin (version 3.6.5), the product reference selects a drawing on a PDF page. The template instructions give the pivot in millimetres from the lower-left corner of the page. OsiriX keeps the physical scale when it crops the drawing and converts the drawing’s resolution to millimetres per pixel when it places the overlay. The catalogue size selects the model. The plugin does not stretch the shell to match that number. Original template instructions · Overlay sizing in version 3.6.5.

The X-ray has its own scale. In OsiriX you can draw a line across an object of known length and enter that length. PXM fits a circle to three points on the calibration ball. When both measure the same ball diameter, they apply the same magnification correction to a radiograph with known pixel spacing. OsiriX image calibration.

PXM preserves the PDF scale through the 300 dpi conversion and checks it against the ruler or other reference on each sheet. Across all 705 AP sheets (794 catalogue entries), the largest difference between PXM’s stored scale and the original page scale was under 0.01 mm over a 40 mm interval. This comparison covers the drawing scale before either application renders the implant on screen.

Place the overlay on a radiograph

  1. Establish the image scale. PXM uses patient DICOM Pixel Spacing when available. It treats row spacing as the height of a pixel and column spacing as its width, since they can differ. If the file supplies only detector spacing, or is an ordinary picture without patient spacing, fit a calibration ball of known diameter before placing a template.
  2. Fit the ball when available. Enter its physical diameter and place three points on its boundary. PXM applies one correction for magnification. An existing template changes size around its pivot when calibration changes.
  3. Select the side, family and size. In Planning, cups and cages appear under Place Cup Template. Whole stems and separate proximal or distal components appear under Place Stem Template. Check the radiograph's own side marker. PXM mirrors the drawing when needed and offers side-specific references only on the permitted side.
  4. Place and adjust. Click to place the pivot. Drag the placed implant to move it. Drag the round handle or enter a rotation angle to turn it around the pivot. Zero degrees follows the drawing's original AP orientation. Each side can hold one acetabular and one femoral template at a time.

Worked example with a 25 mm ball. If the ball measures 20 image units before correction, each unit represents 25 ÷ 20 = 1.25 mm. A 40 mm template interval then spans 32 image units. PXM's Line result reads 32.00 su | 40.00 mm. Use the millimetre value to check the drawing. The su value is the distance in the image's scene coordinates.

How size was verified

First check the ruler. Across the 296 pages with a printed ruler, the distance from 0 to 40 mm in the full PNGs measured 39.977–40.005 mm at the sheet scale. The width of the ink and the finite pixel size affect exactly where an endpoint falls.

Then check the drawing. For all 157 cups, width was measured between the outer shell strokes, in the direction parallel to the cup opening. Construction lines, dots and marks from another size on the same page were excluded. The local transparency pattern at all 314 endpoints matched between the source crop and the packaged drawing. A separate check on 51 straight distal stems compared opposing body walls at a fixed level 30 mm above the tip.

Finally check the application. Both editions were tested on a synthetic DICOM radiograph with unequal horizontal and vertical spacing. Each edition returned 32 direct Line readings across both hips, changes in rotation and calibration. On the two example drawings above, a 40 mm reference interval read 40.00 mm. These are checks of software geometry and preservation of the drawings. Clinical performance requires separate validation.

Make your own overlay pack

Import overlay pack… reads a local UTF-8 .json file describing AP outlines in millimetres. The bundled catalogue uses prepared PNG images. The session importer accepts one closed outline for each item. A PDF, PNG or ZIP file cannot be selected as an overlay pack.

Download example packDownload JSON Schema

The example contains one synthetic stem with vertices specified directly in millimetres. You can import it as supplied and use it as a starting file. The schema checks file structure. PXM also checks geometry and existing identifiers.

Prepare the outline

  1. Choose an AP drawing. Identify the exact product, size and side. Establish scale from a ruler, a stated actual-size sheet or documented dimensions. Record the source and how you verified the scale.
  2. Choose the pivot, usually a documented centre or other reproducible landmark. Express it and every point in the same frame. Positive x goes laterally, away from the pelvic midline on the intended hip. Positive y goes superiorly, towards the patient's head. PXM subtracts the pivot from each vertex. You may set the pivot to [0, 0] and enter all points relative to it.
  3. Follow the exterior in order and close the loop by repeating the first pair. Leave out text, crosshairs, internal holes and separate pieces. If measuring from a PNG, convert pixels to millimetres on both axes. For an upright picture with lateral to the right, a point 20 pixels right and 10 pixels below the pivot at 0.1 mm per pixel becomes [2, -1]. Reverse the horizontal sign if lateral points left in your source.

Fill in the JSON

Keep the four header values exactly as shown. Add one item per distinct outline or size to the items list. Text values, including sizes, go in quotation marks. Coordinates are numbers.

{
  "format": "pxm-overlay-pack-1",
  "units": "mm",
  "view": "AP",
  "axes": "x-lateral-y-superior",
  "items": [{
    "id": "example/asymmetric-stem/1",
    "manufacturer": "PXM demonstration",
    "family": "Synthetic demonstration",
    "size": "1",
    "reference": "PXM-DEMO-1",
    "source": "Constructed geometry for software testing",
    "scale_method": "Vertices specified directly in millimetres",
    "component": "stem",
    "side": "both",
    "pivot": [0,0],
    "outline": [[-6,5],[7,10],[8,-18],[2,-41],[-1,-41],[-4,-10],[-6,5]]
  }]
}
What to enter for each item
FieldMeaning
idA stable, unique identifier for this geometry and product variant. A different geometry, size or permitted side needs a new ID so that older plans can reopen correctly.
manufacturer, family, size, referenceGive the manufacturer, product family, size or variant label, and exact product reference. These distinguish entries that look alike.
source, scale_methodIdentify the drawing and revision, and describe the actual ruler, ratio or known dimensions used.
component, sideUse cup or stem and use left, right or both. Choose both only for a design appropriate to either hip.
pivot, outlineGive the two pivot coordinates and the ordered list of exterior coordinate pairs. The first and last pair must match exactly.

Check the limits before importing

  • Each file can contain 1–1,500 items and be up to 12 MiB (12,582,912 bytes). Each required text field must contain a visible, non-whitespace value, be at most 512 characters long and contain no control characters.
  • Each outline needs 4–4,096 pairs including the repeated closing point, and must enclose at least 1 mm². Use finite numeric coordinates within ±2,000 mm for the pivot and outline. After subtracting the pivot, each coordinate must still be within that range.
  • Give each vertex once around the perimeter, then close the loop. Crossing edges, non-adjacent edges that touch, and repeated intermediate vertices cause an import error.
  • Use each ID only once per file. PXM can reuse an ID already in the session if the outline relative to the pivot, family, size, component and side agree. If an item is invalid or conflicts with an existing item, PXM rejects the whole file and keeps the previous catalogue. Correct the reported item and retry.

Import a pack and reopen a plan

  1. In Planning, choose Import overlay pack… and select your JSON file. PXM reads it on your device. You can open How It Works beside the button while your current case stays open.
  2. Set the active side and open the matching cup or stem selector. For the example pack, look in Place Stem Template for Synthetic demonstration, size 1. If an item is missing, check its component, permitted side and any import error.
  3. Test one size on a calibrated image before using the rest. Check a known physical distance with Line, inspect a recognisable asymmetric feature on each permitted side, and rotate about the intended pivot. Keep a note of the pack revision with your research measurements.
  4. Imported items remain available as you move between cases in that session. CSV and MRB files retain the template ID, position and rotation. Keep the original pack and reimport it in a new session to restore the matching drawing. An annotated PNG records its appearance.
